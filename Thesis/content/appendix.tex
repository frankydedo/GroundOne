% Source: Project/outputs/results/*/predictions.parquet (LLM judge texts stored by the latency and energy runs),

This appendix shows what each system returns for the same answer of T1, so that the numbers of
Chapter~\ref{sec:results} can be related to concrete outputs. The answer (identifier \texttt{rt-7784}) is a data-to-text
answer written by a language model from a structured description of a restaurant: a JSON record with its name, address,
categories, opening hours (Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00--21:00), attributes, an average rating of 4.5 stars and two customer
reviews. It was chosen because every system scored it and because it contains all three classes; it was not selected
for the performance of any system. Its six sentences and their reference labels are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Mesa Verde is a popular business located in Santa Barbara, CA.} --- supported.
\item \emph{It offers a variety of services including Garage Door Services, Restaurants, Live/Raw Food, \dots, Vegan, and
Salad.} --- supported.
\item \emph{The business is open seven days a week from 11 am to 9 pm.} --- contradicted (it is closed on Mondays).
\item \emph{Mesa Verde has a rating of 4.5 stars on Google and has received multiple positive reviews.} --- not mentioned
(the record gives no source for the rating and contains two reviews, one of them negative).
\item \emph{One customer praised the restaurant's fresh, delicious, and inventive cuisine, while another was
disappointed by the taste and price of the food.} --- supported.
\item \emph{Despite this, many customers have had positive experiences at Mesa Verde and recommend it for its fusion and
vegan food options.} --- supported.
\end{enumerate}

The 0.5B GroundOne gets all six sentences right. The contradiction in sentence~3 is caught with a calibrated
$P(\text{supported})$ of 0.50: well below the threshold, but far from certain, which is what a calibrated probability
should express for a sentence that is correct except for one day of the week. The 3B GroundOne also catches both errors
but serves sentence~6, a generalisation over the two reviews, as not mentioned: its $P(\text{supported})$ of 0.85 is
below the high threshold that the base rate of RAGTruth imposes, a case where a user who prefers fewer flags would lower
the threshold. With the same weights, the generated token (B2) gives the right letter on five of the six sentences, but
on the remaining one it produces a space (0.5B) or the word ``We'' (3B) instead of a verdict. The binary encoders separate the two errors
well but also reject sentences~5 or~6, which summarise the reviews in different words; Rizzo-Flow accepts every
sentence. The LLM judges fail in the ways that Chapter~\ref{sec:results} quantifies, as the raw outputs below
show.
