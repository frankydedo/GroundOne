# Analysis of results so far and decisions (2026-10-06)

Scope: everything measured up to the morning of 2026-10-06. That is the first GroundOne model (0.5B, data v1) and the baselines B1, B3 and B4 on the full test sets (Rizzo-Flow on subsamples). Raw results are in `Project/results/`, figures in `Project/figures/` (groups `training-g1-05b-v1`, `diagnostics-g1-05b-v1`, `results`, `phase0`, `sweep`).

**In one sentence:** on the plain "document + one sentence" task GroundOne-0.5B already reaches the zero-shot 7B. However, the two things that make it a *System One specialist* were not learned with data v1. Those are the multi-sentence format, with K questions over one shared answer, and the rules written in the prompt. The diagnosis points to the training data, not to the model or the optimiser, so data v2 is proposed before any further training.

## 3. Diagnosis

### 3.1 The multi-sentence format costs accuracy, and long answers break it
- **Same sentences, two formats** (figure `diagnostics-g1-05b-v1/format_effect_same_sentences.png`). On the 15,224 RAGTruth sentences that also appear in LLM-AggreFact, with LLM-AggreFact labels:
  - document + one sentence (pair format): **0.713**;
  - document + whole numbered answer + "question about sentence *i*" (System One format): **0.656**.
  - The model did learn the task, but less so in the format that gives the System One its speed advantage.
- **Answer length** (figure `accuracy_by_position_and_K.png`). Accuracy falls with K:
  - 0.69 for K ≤ 4 and 0.66 for K 5–8;
  - 0.60 for K 9–13 and **0.47 for K ≥ 14** (below chance).
  - By position, the first sentence (0.55) and sentences after the 6th (0.51) are the worst.
  - Referring to "sentence [12]" inside a long numbered answer is hard for a 0.5B model that rarely saw it in training: only RAGTruth rows (18 % of the data) used the multi-sentence format, and they were sampled one sentence at a time.

### 3.2 The rules and the three-way question were learned as label priors, not as conditions
Figure `predicted_answer_shares.png`:
- **Lenient rule:** the model answers *acceptable* for **99.6 %** of RAGTruth sentences. The dev accuracy of 0.83 shown during training was an artefact of plain accuracy on a mostly-acceptable dev set; balanced accuracy is at chance.
- **Three-way:** *not mentioned* is predicted for **0.02 %** of sentences.
- **Strict** behaves like the binary question with a different bias. Its fitted temperature is 2.96 on the best checkpoint and 12.8 on the final one, which means confident but uninformative logits.
- **Likely cause: one question per row.** In v1 each (document, sentence) pair appears with *one* random question. The model never sees the same pair answered differently under the strict and the lenient rule, so nothing forces it to read the rule. The cheapest solution under that data is to learn a prior per question ("lenient → mostly acceptable", since 2/3 of lenient labels are A), and that is what it did.

### 3.3 Probabilities are squeezed around 0.5 and shifted per task
Figure `probability_spread_by_task.png`. In the multi-sentence format P(supported) has a standard deviation of only 0.02–0.03, and its mean differs by task: 0.54 for QA, 0.52 for Summary, **0.45 for data-to-text**.
- Ranking is better than the accuracy suggests: AUROC is 0.74 on QA and 0.69 on data-to-text.
- The fixed 0.5 threshold falls on the wrong side of a small bias. Data-to-text (89 % supported) is predicted mostly *unsupported*, hence 0.51 balanced accuracy.
- Temperature scaling (T = 2.25 for `sup`) flattens these logits further. The same effect makes the pooled ECE on LLM-AggreFact high (0.29).

### 3.4 Training dynamics
Figure `training-g1-05b-v1/dev_accuracy_by_question.png`:
- the dev loss reached its minimum at step 250 (11 % of the epoch) and then oscillated;
- the binary question kept improving slowly (0.60 → 0.65);
- the rule and three-way curves are noisy.

This matches §3.2. With v1 data there is little left to learn after the first few hundred steps, because the signal that would teach the rules is missing. Dev selection by loss on a dev set with one random question per item is also noisy.

### 3.5 What this means for the research questions
| RQ | Status after v1 |
| --- | --- |
| RQ1 specialisation | **Supported on the pair format** (0.5B: 64.2 → 72.0 on LLM-AggreFact, ≈ zero-shot 7B). Not yet in the multi-sentence format |
| RQ2 speed (System One vs generative judges) | Supported by Phase 0b (gain grows with K). Speed is only useful if accuracy holds at high K, which v1 does not provide (§3.1) |
| RQ3 calibration | Not supported yet: squeezed, task-shifted probabilities (§3.3) |
| RQ4 on-device cost | Fine: training peak 4.3 GB, inference well within 16 GB |
| RQ5 flexibility (rules) | **Not supported with v1** (§3.2). Rizzo-Flow currently follows the rules better than GroundOne |

## 4. Decisions (proposed; the author decides)

### D20 — Data v2: contrastive questions
For every training item with a three-way label (ANLI, RAGTruth), include **all four questions** as separate rows: supported?, three-way, strict and lenient. The same (document, sentence) then appears with answers that differ only because the question or rule differs, which is the supervision the model lacked. To keep the token budget similar, use fewer distinct items.

### D21 — Data v2: much more multi-sentence training, including long answers
- **ANLI as synthetic multi-sentence answers.** Group 3–10 hypotheses of the same training premise (all premises are disjoint from dev/test by construction) into one numbered "answer", each with its human three-way label. The model then practises "question about sentence [i]" with clean labels, and with K up to 10.
- **RAGTruth full answers.** Use all sentences of a selection of training answers, both hallucinated and clean, instead of single sampled sentences, including long answers (K ≥ 14) within the 2,048-token cap.
- MiniCheck synthetic data stays in pair format for the binary question.
- Target: about **50 %** of training rows in the multi-sentence format (v1: 18 %).

### D22 — Dev set and checkpoint selection
A fixed dev set where every item carries all four questions. The selection metric is the **mean balanced accuracy across questions and formats**, not loss or plain accuracy. It is logged per question and format, and the best checkpoint is chosen on it.

### D23 — Calibration with a bias term
Fit, per question type, a temperature **and a bias** (Platt scaling on the logit difference) on the dev set **in the multi-sentence format**. This corrects the per-task shift seen in §3.3. Report balanced accuracy at the fixed 0.5 threshold (comparability) and at the dev-chosen threshold, plus ECE before and after.

### D24 — Optional: negation balance in ANLI training rows
Subsample ANLI training rows so that the share of negation words is similar across classes. The claim-only baseline on v1 training rows reaches 0.56. This is lower priority than D20–D21.

### D25 — Order of the next runs
- **Tonight: retrain the 0.5B on v2** (about 7–8 h, same hyper-parameters), not the 3B. It is the cheaper test of whether D20–D22 fix the problems.
- **Tomorrow night: the 3B on v2**, if the 0.5B v2 improves. If it does not, we revisit before spending a night of 3B training.
- **Keep v1 results.** v1 vs v2 becomes an ablation in the thesis: what contrastive questions and multi-sentence training contribute.

### Reporting rules confirmed by this analysis
- Report RAGTruth on the sentences shared with LLM-AggreFact, with its labels, as the primary number; the full-sentence number is secondary.
- Always report per question with balanced accuracy, and the share of each predicted answer, to catch "always the same answer" behaviour.
- For Rizzo-Flow, the subsamples must be enlarged before the final tables (currently 600 / 200 / 1,100 items).
