# Qu 2024 – Mobile Edge Intelligence for Large Language Models: A Contemporary Survey

**Raw file**: `raw/local_inference/Qu 2024 - Mobile Edge Intelligence for Large Language Models, A Contemporary Survey (arXiv 2407.18921)_ingested.pdf`

## Overview
A 42-page survey from the wireless-communications community (HKU, IEEE authors) on **mobile edge intelligence (MEI)**: running LLMs not in the cloud and not only on the device, but on **edge servers of the mobile network** (base stations, access points) in cooperation with devices, framed for 6G. It motivates edge deployment with four "killer applications" (§II), reviews resource-efficient LLM techniques for inference and fine-tuning (§IV), proposes an architecture called **MEI4LLM** (§V), and then covers edge **caching and delivery** of models (§VI), edge **training** (centralised, federated, split, hierarchical; §VII) and edge **inference** (centralised, split, collaborative; §VIII), ending with green, secure and quality-aware edge LLMs (§IX). Its distinguishing angle is **communication efficiency**, which it says earlier efficiency surveys overlook (§I-C, Table I).

## Key Insights
1. **Three-tier framing (§I-B, §III-B).** Cloud LLMs suffer from privacy breaches, bandwidth costs and latency; on-device LLMs are limited by compute, memory and storage, so industry focuses on **sub-10B** models. Gemini Nano (1.8B and 3.25B, 4-bit) supports only "basic" functions such as summarisation, smart replies and grammar checks (§I-B). MEI sits in between: more capable than devices, closer than the cloud.
2. **Motivations for edge/on-device inference.** Abstract: on-device LLMs are "more cost-effective, latency-efficient, and privacy-preserving" than cloud. §IV-A: "On-device LLM inference eliminates privacy leakage and the need for Internet connections." §V-D: on-device inference "preserves privacy and eliminates communication costs" but imposes the full compute load; on-server inference requires uploading raw data. Application requirements (Fig. 2): mobile health ≤400 ms, humanoid robots 10–100 ms, virtual assistants 200 ms, autonomous driving 10 ms.
3. **On-device challenges, with numbers (§IV).** Llama 2 7B needs about **1,700 GFLOPs** for one forward pass on 128 tokens, against **45 TOPS** of the Hexagon NPU in the Snapdragon X Elite; Adam training needs about **12×** the memory of inference; a 13B INT4 model in llama.cpp on a Xiaomi 11 consumes about **56 J/token**, so a 3,000 mAh / 3.7 V battery would yield only about **700 tokens**.
4. **Technique taxonomy (Fig. 7, §IV-A/B).** Resource-efficient inference = *LLM compression* (quantization PTQ/QAT, structured/unstructured pruning, white-box/black-box distillation) + *fast decoding* (speculative decoding, early exit, mixture-of-experts, contextual sparsity, parallel decoding, sparse attention, KV-cache optimisation). Resource-efficient fine-tuning = PEFT (additive, selective, reparameterised such as LoRA) + zeroth-order optimisation.
5. **Why weight-only quantization (§IV-A1).** Most on-device methods use PTQ because QAT needs retraining. LLM quantization focuses on weights only because activation quantization degrades more and because "the primary sources of latency and energy consumption when generating new tokens … are often due to loading model parameters from memory". AWQ keeps about 1% of critical weights in FP16 and quantizes the rest to INT3/INT4.
6. **KV cache (§IV-A2).** The KV cache grows with generated tokens; compression and eviction examples: 2-bit KV quantization cuts peak memory 2.6×; MiniCache −41% memory with 4-bit; Scissorhands 5× less KV memory; another method keeps performance with 12% of the KV cache. Loading parameters from disk when the model exceeds memory can account for up to **90%** of inference latency; speculative decoding halves per-token latency and energy in the cited study.
7. **Real products (§IV-A2).** Gemini Nano is distilled from larger Gemini models and quantized to 4 bits; Apple Intelligence uses speculative decoding among other techniques.
8. **Memory bandwidth gap (§VII-B).** Deep-learning accelerators reach up to **7.8 TB/s** memory bandwidth, embedded edge devices up to **0.2 TB/s** (cited in the context of federated training).
9. **Collaborative inference with confidence (§VIII-C, §IX-A).** Device–server speculative decoding: the device drafts, the server verifies; devices can skip upload when **calibrated confidence** is high. For green AI, a small model gives the initial result and the cloud LLM is invoked "only when the inference confidence is low". Energy table (Table VII): GPT-3 training 1,287 MWh, GPT-4 ≥50,000 MWh; daily response energy 3 and 5 MWh.
10. **RAG at the edge (§III-A4, §VI-A3).** RAG reduces hallucinations by grounding on fresh sources; the survey proposes caching popular knowledge sources on edge servers and jointly deciding what to fine-tune versus what to cache.
11. **Open challenges (§IX).** Green edge LLMs ("AI energy efficiency" = accuracy per energy), secure edge LLMs (inference attacks on split/federated learning, poisoning of adapters under PEFT), quality-aware edge training with noisy, unlabelled edge data.

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **Where GroundOne sits.** In the survey's taxonomy GroundOne is pure **on-device inference** (§V-D), not MEI: no edge server, no split, no collaboration. It uses two techniques from Fig. 7, weight quantization (8-bit MLX) and reparameterised PEFT (LoRA), and none of the "fast decoding" family, because it does not decode. The survey's own focus (network-assisted LLMs, 6G) is *not* GroundOne's position, and the thesis should cite it for background and motivation, not as support for a design choice.
- **Locality pillar.** Useful citable motivations: on-device inference "eliminates privacy leakage and the need for Internet connections" (§IV-A), plus privacy-sensitive scenarios (health data under GDPR, §II). The paper itself argues that pure on-device deployment is too limited for complex tasks, which is a counter-position the thesis should acknowledge: GroundOne answers it by narrowing the task (one verification decision per sentence), not by moving computation to a server ([[On-Device Inference]]).
- **Prefill/decode argument.** The survey explicitly locates the cost of on-device generation in **loading weights from memory for every new token** (§IV-A1) and frames KV-cache growth as a decode-phase problem (§IV-A2). This supports the thesis claim that removing decoding removes the memory-bound part of the cost ([[Prefill and Decode]], [[KV Cache]]). The 56 J/token figure is a vivid (if old-hardware) illustration for [[Energy of LLM Inference]].
- **Verification and classification.** The survey does not discuss hallucination detection or groundedness verification on device; "verification" in §IV-A2 and §VIII-C means the speculative-decoding verifier. The closest idea is **confidence-gated escalation** (§VIII-C, §IX-A), which parallels GroundOne's calibrated threshold: a calibrated local verifier could decide when a second opinion is needed ([[Calibration]], [[Selective Prediction]]). No classification readout or logit-only decision model is discussed.
