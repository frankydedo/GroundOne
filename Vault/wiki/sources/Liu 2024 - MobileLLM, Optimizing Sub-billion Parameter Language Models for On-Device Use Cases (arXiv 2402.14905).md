# Liu 2024 – MobileLLM: Optimizing Sub-billion Parameter Language Models for On-Device Use Cases

**Raw file**: `raw/local_inference/Liu 2024 - MobileLLM, Optimizing Sub-billion Parameter Language Models for On-Device Use Cases (arXiv 2402.14905)_ingested.pdf`

## Overview
A Meta paper arguing that LLMs for phones should have **fewer than a billion parameters**, because of DRAM capacity and energy per token, and that at this scale **architecture matters more than the scaling-law view suggests**. Starting from a 12-layer, 768-dimensional 125M baseline, it combines SwiGLU FFNs, a **deep-and-thin** shape, input–output **embedding sharing** and **grouped-query attention** into **MobileLLM**. It then adds **immediate block-wise weight sharing** (each block computed twice in a row) to get **MobileLLM-LS**, without growing the model. The models gain 2.7/4.3 points (MobileLLM) on zero-shot commonsense tasks over previous 125M/350M state of the art, plus 0.7/0.8 points from layer sharing (abstract). They are trained from scratch on 1T tokens on 32 A100s (§2.1, §3.1).

## Key Insights
1. **Mobile memory budget (§1, Fig. 2).** Phone DRAM is 6–12 GB (iPhone 15: 6 GB; Pixel 8 Pro: 12 GB). Because DRAM is shared with the OS and other apps, the authors state that **an app should not exceed 10% of DRAM**. Fig. 2 sketches the hierarchy as SRAM 8–32 MB at ~100 GB/s, DRAM 6–12 GB at ~10 GB/s, and flash ~100 GB at ~1 GB/s. A LLaMA-v2 7B with 8-bit weights is called "prohibitively expensive" on phones.
2. **Energy per token (§1).** Taking **0.1 J/token per billion parameters** as LLM energy, a 7B model uses **0.7 J/token**. An iPhone battery holds about **50 kJ**, enough for less than **2 hours** of conversation at 10 tokens/s, and every **64 tokens drain 0.2%** of the battery. A **350M 8-bit model at 0.035 J/token** could support conversational use for an entire day. The 0.1 J/token/B figure comes from cited prior work (Han et al. 2016; Malladi et al. 2012), not from measurements in this paper.
3. **Decode speed (§1).** The 125M model reaches **50 tokens/s**; the iPhone app MLC Chat runs LLaMA 7B at **3–6 tokens/s** (cited from llm.mlc.ai, not measured by the authors).
4. **Cloud cost motivation (§1).** If people spent about 5% of their daily time on LLMs, serving GPT-4 at 50 tokens/s would need about **one hundred million H100s** (60 TFLOP/s each), comparable to 160 Meta-scale companies (calculation in their appendix).
5. **Architecture findings (§2.2, Fig. 3–5, Table 1).** SwiGLU raises the 125M average from 42.6 to 43.9. Over 19 models, depth beats width: 30–42 layers beat 12 layers at 125M. Embedding sharing removes 16M parameters (11.8%) for −0.2 points, recovered by two extra layers (+0.4 with still 10M fewer parameters). GQA works best with 16 query heads; reducing kv-heads from 16 to 4 keeps accuracy at 125M and costs 0.2 points at 350M for about 10% fewer parameters.
6. **Layer sharing and memory movement (§2.3, Table 2, Table 7).** SRAM (about 20 MB) holds roughly one transformer block, so computing the same block twice in a row avoids reloading weights from DRAM. On an iPhone 13 (ExecuTorch, Metal Performance Shaders, FP16), MobileLLM-125M takes 39.2 ms to load, 1361.7 ms to initialise and 15.6 ms to execute. MobileLLM-LS (2×30 layers, shared) takes 43.6 / 1388.2 / 16.0 ms (+2.2% load and init, +2.6% execution). A non-shared 60-layer model takes 68.6 / 3347.7 / 29.0 ms (+143% load and init, +86% execution). The paper does not say whether "execute" is one forward pass or one token.
7. **Results (Tables 3–6).** Zero-shot average: MobileLLM-125M 46.3, LS-125M 47.0, MobileLLM-350M 51.3, LS-350M 52.1. Chat: MobileLLM-LS-350M has an AlpacaEval win rate of 48.2% against text-davinci-001 (MT-Bench 3.16). **API calling** (Table 6): MobileLLM-350M reaches EM_intent 65.3 and EM_structure 48.8 vs LLaMA-v2 7B at 62.8 / 50.9, while lagging on response ROUGE (46.8/44.6 vs 56.5/54.3). The authors conclude that common on-device tasks "are not particularly challenging" and small fine-tuned models handle them.
8. **Quantisation and distillation (§3.4–3.5, Fig. 7).** W8A8 per-token min-max PTQ costs **less than 0.5 points**. Knowledge distillation from LLaMA-v2 7B made training 2.6–3.2× slower and gave comparable or worse accuracy than label training.

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **The sub-billion argument applies to the 0.5B verifier.** Qwen2.5-0.5B is in the size class MobileLLM targets and already uses its recipe: SwiGLU, tied embeddings, GQA with 14 query / 2 KV heads, 24 layers at hidden size 896, so relatively deep and thin ([[Qwen2.5]], [[Small Language Models]]). MobileLLM's API-calling result supports the thesis claim that a narrow, fine-tuned task (sentence-level verification) does not need a large general model.
- **The 10%-of-DRAM rule on the thesis machine.** On a 16 GB M4, 10% is 1.6 GB. The 0.5B verifier (about 0.5 GB of 8-bit weights) fits that budget as a background component next to the RAG generator; the 3B (about 3.3 GB) does not. This is a concrete, citable argument for the 0.5B as the deployable default and the 3B as the accuracy ceiling ([[On-Device Inference]], [[Apple Silicon and MLX]]). The rule is a phone heuristic; applying it to a laptop is the thesis author's extrapolation.
- **Energy per token versus a zero-token verifier.** By the paper's rule of thumb (0.1 J/token/B), decoding costs about 0.05 J/token at 0.5B and 0.3 J/token at 3B on a phone. GroundOne generates **no tokens**, so the per-token decode term disappears and only one prefill remains. The thesis measurement on the M4 (SoC power) is 5.5 J (0.5B) and 33.1 J (3B) per verified answer of K ≥ 14 sentences, vs 22.5 / 111.7 J for a verbose generative judge ([[Energy of LLM Inference]], [[GroundOne Results]]). The rule of thumb ignores prefill and the hardware differs, so the two sets of numbers illustrate scale, not a like-for-like comparison.
- **Bandwidth framing.** Insight 6 is the same mechanism as the decode roofline: per-token latency is dominated by moving weights from DRAM ([[Prefill and Decode]]). The Fig. 2 DRAM figure (~10 GB/s) is far below the M4's 120 GB/s; it should be read as schematic and not cited as a phone bandwidth figure.
- **Not applicable.** The paper is about pretraining architectures from scratch; GroundOne fine-tunes an existing model with LoRA and changes no architecture. Its latency numbers are FP16 on an iPhone 13 and do not transfer to MLX on the M4.
