# Wang 2025 – Cognitive Edge Computing: A Comprehensive Survey on Optimizing Large Models and AI Agents for Pervasive Deployment

**Raw file**: `raw/local_inference/Optimizing Edge AI, A Comprehensive Survey (arXiv 2501.03265)_ingested.pdf`

## Overview
A 65-page survey on deploying "reasoning-capable" LLMs and AI agents on edge devices, under the label **Cognitive Edge Computing**. It organises the field into (1) model optimisation (quantization, sparsity, low-rank adaptation, distillation), (2) system architecture (on-device inference, offloading, cloud–edge collaboration) and (3) adaptive intelligence (context compression, routing, federated personalisation) (abstract), and structures solutions as data, model and system optimisation (§4, Fig. 3). It adds application catalogues, an evaluation protocol (§6.7), research gaps (§6.8) and a "threats to validity" section (§7). The authors disclose AI-assisted tools for literature organisation, grammar and initial draft structuring (Acknowledgments).

## Key Insights
1. **Three computing tiers (§1, Table 1).** Cloud LLMs (>175B; 100 ms–2 s latency including network; 100–1000 W), edge servers (7–70B; 50–200 ms; 20–100 W), **on-device SLMs (1–3B; 10–100 ms "pure local"; sub-10 W; <1 MB–8 GB compressed)**. Connectivity: always required / intermittent / optional. The table's values are labelled "typical implementation ranges" without per-cell sources.
2. **Why leave the cloud (§3.1).** Three limitations of cloud-centric AI: *network dependency* (round trips of 50–500 ms; no service when offline, e.g. rural areas, aircraft), *privacy and data sovereignty* (uploading conversations and documents; GDPR/HIPAA cross-border limits; corporate and government data must stay within controlled boundaries), and *limited personalisation*. On-device benefits listed in §1: low latency, offline availability, energy efficiency, privacy, personalisation.
3. **Memory and bandwidth constraints (§3.2).** Smartphones offer 6–12 GB DRAM. Autoregressive generation can saturate memory bandwidth: an NVIDIA A100 has 1,555 GB/s, mobile SoCs about 50–60 GB/s (Snapdragon 8 Gen 2), "an order-of-magnitude difference" that slows token generation. §4.2.1: edge transformer inference is "frequently memory-bandwidth-bound and thermally limited"; throughput "depends more on data movement than on peak TOPS", favouring weight-only or mixed-precision quantization while keeping precision where sensitivity is high ("e.g., attention, logits").
4. **Compression (§4).** INT8/INT4 quantization gives 4–8× compression (relative to FP32); methods named: QLoRA, GPTQ, AWQ, HAQ, W4A16, AdaRound/BRECQ. Single techniques give early gains but **stacking them shows diminishing returns** (§8 conclusion).
5. **Small language models (§4.2).** Parameters "from millions to billions"; advantages: faster deployment cycles, easier fine-tuning on proprietary data, lower energy, suitability for constrained devices, and "stronger privacy/control" for enterprise use. Architecture tailoring (MobileLLM deep-narrow; MobileLLM-R1 950M reported above Qwen3 0.6B on maths/coding benchmarks) "generally yields better density than naïve downscaling".
6. **Runtime and KV cache (§4.3.2).** Paged KV cache (vLLM), continuous batching, KV compression/eviction, KV persistence across app invocations (LLM as a mobile OS service), offloading (FlexGen), speculative decoding.
7. **Energy reporting (§3.5).** Distinguish device power (W), facility power, and energy per request (Wh/query); report both steady-state TDP and measured Wh/query with workload and hardware; in lifecycle terms, tokens per joule and embodied vs operational energy.
8. **Measurement protocol (§6.7) and gaps (§6.8, §7).** Report prompt and generation length, precision, KV policy, batching, device and thermal state, energy-meter scope, code version and seeds. Gaps: unified edge benchmarks, reproducible energy reporting, edge safety/alignment evaluation, multi-agent testbeds. Threats to validity include heterogeneous testbeds, publication bias and inconsistent energy measurement.
9. **Apple ecosystem (§5.3).** Mentions MLX "optimized primitives for machine learning on Apple Silicon … unified memory architecture" and the M4 Neural Engine at 38 TOPS.

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **Where GroundOne sits.** In Table 1, GroundOne is in the **on-device tier** (0.5B and 3B, "pure local", connectivity optional), and in §4 it uses model optimisation only (8-bit quantization + LoRA), with no system-level partitioning or collaboration. The survey's emphasis on "reasoning-capable" models and agents is the opposite of GroundOne's narrow verifier.
- **Locality pillar.** §3.1 is a compact, citable list of reasons to stay local (offline availability, privacy, data sovereignty/regulation, controlled boundaries for proprietary data), directly applicable to verifying RAG answers over private documents ([[On-Device Inference]]). Cite it as a secondary source, ideally next to a stronger one.
- **Bandwidth argument.** §3.2 and §4.2.1 support the claim that edge LLM throughput is limited by memory movement during generation, which a decode-free model sidesteps ([[Prefill and Decode]], [[Apple Silicon and MLX]]). **Caution:** §4.3.2 states that prefill is "bandwidth-bound", which contradicts the standard account (prefill compute-bound, decode bandwidth-bound; [[Pope 2023 - Efficiently Scaling Transformer Inference|Pope 2023]], [[Das 2026 - Large Models for Small Devices, Edge AI Deployment (arXiv 2608.15693)|Das 2026]] §6.3). Do not cite this survey for the prefill/decode distinction.
- **Energy methodology.** §3.5 and §6.7 (Wh/query, meter scope, workload description) match how GroundOne reports energy per verification with `tools/power.py`, and can be cited for the reporting protocol ([[Energy of LLM Inference]]).
- **Verification/hallucination.** Not addressed beyond passing mentions ("varying hallucination rates", §3.4; data cleaning to reduce hallucination risk, §4.1). Encoder-only models for "classification" at the edge are mentioned (§4.2, mmBERT), but no classification readout from a decoder or decision-model concept.
- **Quality caveats (significant).** Preprint with AI-assisted drafting disclosed. Breadth over depth, promotional language ("revolutionary", "breakthrough"), and Wikipedia and vendor pages as sources. Errors and weak spots: §3.2 says 10B models need "up to 20GB" with INT8 (INT8 is about 1 byte per weight, i.e. about 10 GB); §4.3.2 calls prefill bandwidth-bound; §1 lists "Tiny-random-Llama", a random-weight test checkpoint, as an example of an ultra-lightweight design; §3.4 has vague placeholders ("varying hallucination rates") and a garbled sentence; the E1–E3 evidence grading announced in §1.1 is never applied. Use it only for framing statements that are corroborated elsewhere.
