# Dettmers 2022 – LLM.int8(): 8-bit Matrix Multiplication for Transformers at Scale

**Raw file**: `raw/local_inference/Dettmers 2022 - LLM.int8(), 8-bit Matrix Multiplication for Transformers at Scale (arXiv 2208.07339)_ingested.pdf`

## Overview
The first Int8 inference procedure for multi-billion-parameter transformers **without performance degradation**. A 16/32-bit checkpoint of up to 175B parameters can be loaded, its feed-forward and attention projection layers converted to Int8, and used immediately, with **about half the memory** (abstract, §1). The method, LLM.int8(), combines **vector-wise quantisation** with a **mixed-precision decomposition** that keeps a handful of "outlier" feature dimensions in 16-bit. The paper's second contribution is the analysis of these **emergent large-magnitude features**, which explain why earlier 8-bit methods break down from about 6.7B parameters (§4). The software is released as `bitsandbytes` with a Hugging Face Transformers integration (§1). The focus is memory and accessibility, not speed.

## Key Insights
1. **Method in plain words (§3, Fig. 2).** A matrix multiplication is a set of independent inner products, so each row of the hidden states X and each column of the weights W gets its own absmax scaling constant (**vector-wise** quantisation); the Int32 result is rescaled by the outer product of the constants. Any feature dimension containing a value with magnitude ≥ α = 6.0 is split off and multiplied in **fp16**; everything else (more than 99.9 % of values) is multiplied in **Int8** (§3.2, Eq. 8). Since at most 7 outlier dimensions exist for models up to 13B, the decomposition costs about 0.1 % extra memory; BLOOM-176B shrinks by **1.96×** (§3).
2. **What "8-bit" means here: W8A8.** Both weights *and* activations are quantised to Int8, and the matmul runs on Int8 hardware (§2.1, Eq. 6). This is different from weight-only formats, where activations stay in 16-bit (see [[Lin 2024 - AWQ, Activation-aware Weight Quantization for LLM Compression and Acceleration (arXiv 2306.00978)|AWQ]] §2 for the W8A8 vs W4A16 distinction).
3. **Outlier features and the 6.7B phase shift (§4.2, Figs. 3–4, Table 4).** Features up to 20× larger than the rest first appear in about 25 % of layers and spread as models grow; between 6B and 6.7B parameters the share of affected layers jumps from 65 % to 100 % and the share of affected sequence positions from 35 % to 75 %. At 6.7B there are about 150k outliers per 2,048-token sequence, but in only **6 feature dimensions**. Zeroing them halves the mean top-1 attention softmax probability (about 40 % → 20 %) and raises validation perplexity by **600–1000 %**; zeroing 7 random dimensions costs 0.02–0.3 % and about 0.1 %. Measured against **perplexity** rather than size, emergence is smooth and the outlier count rises monotonically as perplexity falls (Fig. 3b, 4b).
4. **Main results (Table 1, C4 perplexity, NVIDIA A40).** At 13B: fp32 12.45; Int8 absmax 19.08; zeropoint 13.94; absmax vector-wise 16.48; LLM.int8() **12.45**. At 6.7B: fp32 13.30; LLM.int8() 13.24. Small models are fragile too: at 125M, fp32 25.65 vs Int8 absmax 87.76 and absmax vector-wise 35.84, while LLM.int8() gives 25.83. Zero-shot accuracy of OPT 125M–175B stays at the 16-bit level, while the 8-bit absmax vector-wise baseline degrades to random from 6.7B (Fig. 1).
5. **Speed: memory saving, not acceleration (§3.4, App. D, Table 5–6).** Int8 matmul only pays off when the GPU is saturated (model dimension ≥ 4096). Relative to fp16, raw Int8 matmul ranges from 0.99× (dim 768) to 2.29× (dim 12288), but with quantisation overhead and decomposition LLM.int8() is a **slowdown** for small dimensions: 0.14× at dim 768, 0.20× at 1024, 0.51× at 2048, 0.86× at 4096, and a speedup only at 13B (1.22×) and 175B (1.81×). End-to-end BLOOM-176B generation per token at batch 1: 239 ms (bf16, 8×A100) vs 246–253 ms (LLM.int8(), 3–8×A100).
6. **Accessibility (Table 2/3).** OPT-175B/BLOOM fits on 8×RTX 3090 (24 GB) in 8-bit vs OPT-66B in 16-bit; on 15 GB Colab Pro the largest runnable model goes from GPT-J-6B to OPT-13B.
7. **Limitations (§6).** Int8 only (no FP8), up to 175B, attention function itself not quantised, inference only (Int8 training studied in App. E).

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **Different 8-bit scheme from GroundOne's.** GroundOne's bases (`lmstudio-community/Qwen2.5-{0.5B,3B}-Instruct-MLX-8bit`) use MLX **weight-only affine** quantisation, 8 bits with **group size 64** (from the models' `config.json`); activations stay in 16-bit. LLM.int8() quantises activations as well. The outlier problem the paper describes is mainly an **activation** problem (outliers sit in feature columns of X, §4.3), so it does not transfer one-to-one. Group-wise weight scales (64 weights per scale) also confine any large weight to its own group, the same "finer granularity" remedy the paper attributes to ZeroQuant/nuQmm (§5).
- **Small models are not automatically safe.** The systematic outliers appear around 6.7B, far above 0.5B/3B, but Table 1 shows that coarse 8-bit schemes hurt **small** models badly too (125M: 25.65 → 35.84 with absmax vector-wise). The paper therefore supports a **measured** claim, not an assumed one: GroundOne should report an 8-bit vs bf16 check on dev (agreement of the readout decision and max |Δp| over A/B/C), as already noted in [[Quantization]] and [[Apple Silicon and MLX]].
- **Logit readout and calibration.** The outliers matter because they shape softmax probabilities (top-1 mass halves without them, §4.2). GroundOne reads three letter logits and turns them into calibrated probabilities ([[Letter-Token Readout]], [[Calibration]]), so a quantisation error that leaves the argmax intact can still move the probabilities and thresholds. The calibration is fitted on the served 8-bit model and its fingerprint includes the base repository (`calibration.py`), so a change of quantisation would force a refit rather than silently shift the served labels.
- **Why 8-bit does not make prefill faster on its own.** LLM.int8() is the case where 8-bit reaches Int8 compute and still slows small layers (0.51× at dim 2048, the hidden size of Qwen2.5-3B; the 0.5B's hidden size of 896 falls between the 0.14× and 0.20× columns; hidden sizes from the models' `config.json`). A weight-only format does even less for compute-bound prefill: the matmul is done in 16-bit after dequantisation, so the savings are bytes moved, which matter for decode ([[Prefill and Decode]]). GroundOne's single forward pass therefore benefits from 8-bit mainly through **memory footprint**, not through FLOPs.
- **Lineage.** The same author's later QLoRA work ([[Dettmers 2023 - QLoRA]]) builds on `bitsandbytes` to train LoRA adapters on a frozen quantised base, the recipe GroundOne follows in MLX ([[LoRA]]).
