# Saad-Falcon 2024 - ARES: An Automated Evaluation Framework for RAG Systems

**Raw file**: `raw/Saad-Falcon 2024 - ARES_ingested.pdf`

## Overview
ARES (Automated RAG Evaluation System) scores RAG systems on **context relevance, answer faithfulness and answer relevance** using **small fine-tuned classifiers** instead of prompted LLMs. Three stages (§3, Fig. 1 p.4):
1. **Synthetic data**: FLAN-T5 XXL generates question–answer pairs from in-domain passages; queries that cannot retrieve their source passage are filtered out. Negatives come from *weak* (random passages/answers) and *strong* (same-document passages, contradictory answers generated by FLAN-T5) strategies (§3.1 p.3).
2. **Judges**: one **DeBERTa-v3-Large (304M) with a binary classifier head** per metric, fine-tuned on the synthetic triples (§3.2, §7).
3. **Prediction-powered inference (PPI)**: a small human-labelled "preference validation set" (~150–300 items) rectifies the judge's predictions on many unlabelled outputs and gives **95% confidence intervals** on each system's score (§3.3).

Inputs required: an in-domain passage set, ~150+ human-annotated datapoints, and ≥5 few-shot in-domain query/answer examples (§1–3).

## Key Insights
1. **Fine-tuned small judge beats prompted LLM judges for ranking.** Across KILT (NQ, HotpotQA, WoW, FEVER) and SuperGLUE (MultiRC, ReCoRD) mock systems, ARES has Kendall's τ on average 0.065 higher than RAGAS for context relevance and 0.132 higher for answer relevance; ~0.06 higher than a few-shot GPT-3.5 judge (§5.1 p.6, Table 1 p.7). Claimed in abstract as "59.3 and 14.4 percentage points" better than RAGAS in *judge accuracy* for context and answer relevance (p.2, p.6).
2. **Per-example judge accuracy (Table 1 p.7)**: e.g. on NQ context relevance, RAGAS 31.4%, GPT-3.5 judge 73.8%, ARES 79.3%; on NQ answer relevance 71.2% / 95.5% / 97.2%.
3. **Faithfulness on real data (AIS)**: on WoW and CNN/DM attribution data, ARES estimates the faithful/hallucinated split within 2.5 points (0.478 vs 0.458; 0.835 vs 0.859), but **per-example judge accuracy is only 62.5% (WoW) and 84.0% (CNN/DM)** (Table 2 p.7). Faithfulness was *not* evaluated on KILT/SuperGLUE because no hallucinated answers were annotated (§4.2 p.5).
4. **PPI** improves ranking accuracy for all datasets vs the judge alone; ARES needs 78% fewer annotations than sampled human annotation (§5.1 p.6). 150 annotations is the minimum that works (§5.1, Table 3).
5. **Real systems**: on NQ/WoW/FEVER with 3 retrievers × 3 LLMs, average τ 0.91 (context rel.) and 0.97 (answer rel.); PPI intervals cover ground truth >95% of the time, mean width 7.4 / 6.1 points (§5.3 p.8).
6. **Domain-shift limits**: judges transfer across query/document types within KILT/SuperGLUE, but fail on drastic shifts: τ 0.33 cross-lingual (XGLUE), 0.28 text-to-code, 0.38 entity extraction (T-Rex) (§5.4 p.8).
7. **Limitations (§7 p.9)**: needs domain-expert annotations (law, medicine, finance); DeBERTa-v3-Large and FLAN-T5-XXL (11.3B) needed GPUs with ~32 GB and "several hours" for fine-tuning/generation; English only.
8. **Cost note**: GPT-3.5 chosen over GPT-4 as baseline "due to the lower financial costs" (Table 1 caption); the GPT-3.5 judge "does come with its own querying costs" but needs no fine-tuning (§5.1 p.6). No per-call latency is reported (not stated).
9. Future work explicitly mentions "utilizing logits in LLM judge prediction to improve PPI confidence intervals" (§6 p.8–9).

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **Closest design precedent**: ARES already argues that a **small fine-tuned classifier** (DeBERTa + binary head) is a better RAG judge than a prompted GPT-3.5. GroundOne makes the same bet with a decoder LM (Qwen2.5 0.5B/3B + LoRA) and a **logit readout of answer letters** instead of an added classifier head. Its future-work note on using judge logits for confidence intervals points directly at GroundOne's probability output and [[Calibration]].
- **Faithfulness granularity**: ARES judges the whole *query–passage–answer triple* (one binary label per answer). GroundOne judges **per sentence**, which localises the unsupported span.
- **Warning for the thesis**: ARES' faithfulness judge is only 62.5–84.0% accurate per example on AIS (insight 3) even though aggregate estimates are good; aggregate-level evaluation can hide weak per-example verification. GroundOne should report per-example metrics (e.g. balanced accuracy on [[LLM-AggreFact]], [[RAGTruth]]).
- **Domain shift**: ARES judges degrade badly outside the training domain (insight 6), a risk also for a LoRA-tuned 0.5B verifier ([[When System One Beats an LLM]], "Distribution shift").
- **Hardware contrast**: ARES needed ~32 GB GPUs; GroundOne trains on a 16 GB M4 Mac with MLX.
