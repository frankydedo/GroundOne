# Laskaridis 2024 – MELTing point: Mobile Evaluation of Language Transformers

**Raw file**: `raw/local_inference/Laskaridis 2024 - MELTing point, Mobile Evaluation of Language Transformers (arXiv 2403.12844)_ingested.pdf`

## Overview
The first systematic measurement study (by the authors' account) of **LLM inference on phones and edge boards**. The authors built **MELT**, an automation and measurement infrastructure (code: github.com/brave-experiments/MELT-public). It downloads, quantises and deploys models, drives real multi-turn chat sessions headlessly on Android, iOS and Jetson, and records latency, memory, **energy** (Monsoon power monitor on battery-bypassed phones, 5 kHz; SysFS probes on Jetson at ~100 Hz) and **temperature** (FLIR thermal camera) (§3–4). Devices: Galaxy S23, Pixel 6a, iPhone 14 Pro, iPhone SE, Jetson Orin AGX and Orin Nano (Table 1). Models: TinyLlama 1.1B, Zephyr-3B, Mistral-7B, Gemma 2B/7B, Llama-2 7B/13B (Table 2). Frameworks: MLC-LLM, llama.cpp and LLMFarm (Table 3). Headline findings: performance is very heterogeneous across devices, **inference is largely memory-bound**, quantisation makes execution viable "but at a non-negligible accuracy cost", and energy and heat make **continuous** on-device LLM use impractical (abstract).

## Key Insights
1. **Prefill vs generation (§5.2.2, Fig. 3).** Prefill throughput is much higher than generation throughput on every device and framework; the authors attribute this to the KV cache and to compute- vs memory-boundedness. The y-axes of Fig. 3a–b span up to about 200 tokens/s for prefill and 30 tokens/s for generation on high-tier phones with MLC-LLM (read from the figure). In a chat, prefill "takes only a small part of the inference which is mostly bottlenecked by the memory-bound generation process" (§5.2.2).
2. **Framework and platform effects (§5.2.2).** MLC-LLM is generally faster than llama.cpp (up to 3.53× in generation) at the cost of per-platform compilation. 4-bit models ran 24.77% faster on average than 3-bit (dequantisation, cache alignment). Metal iPhones had 78.93% higher generation throughput than OpenCL Android phones with MLC. A Mac Studio (M2 Max) ran Llama-2 7B 4-bit at a sustained **46.8 tokens/s** (§2.3).
3. **Energy per token (§5.2.2, Fig. 4–5).** Discharge per token grows with model size, since DRAM traffic and memory copies dominate energy. For Zephyr-3B 4-bit (6 prompts, average input 40 tokens, output 135 tokens), energy was **0.21, 0.20 and 0.16 mWh/token** (≈ 0.76, 0.72, 0.58 J/token) on S23/MLC, iPhone 14 Pro/MLC and iPhone 14 Pro/LLMFarm. A full battery would last **542.78, 490.05 and 590.93 prompts** respectively. Peak power: iPhones up to **13.8 W sustained (average) and 18 W instantaneous**; S23 14 W instantaneous, under 8.5 W sustained.
4. **Per-model table (Appendix Table 5, iPhone 14 Pro, LLMFarm GPU).** TinyLlama-1.1B q4_k: 24.65 tok/s, 0.0079 mAh/token. Zephyr-3B q4_k: 14.82 tok/s, 0.0090 mAh/token. Llama2-7B q3_k: 5.99 tok/s, 0.0528 mAh/token.
5. **Quality of experience (§5.2.3, Fig. 6–7, Fig. 9a).** Model loading takes seconds (Zephyr-3B 4-bit: 2.41 ± 0.09 s, device often unresponsive). The authors also report out-of-memory kills and device restarts. On the iPhone 14 Pro, throughput drops in steps during continuous inference (around prompts 20 and 32, attributed to DVFS or thermal states), and surface temperature reached **47.9 °C**. The Jetson AGX at 50 W stays stable.
6. **Where the time goes (§5.3, Fig. 8–9b).** For Llama-7B 3-bit with MLC on the S23, fused **dequantise + matmul** kernels account for **97%** of prefill and **95.7%** of decode runtime. Apple xctrace memory traces on the iPhone 14 Pro show **GPU compute stalling while waiting for data**, which the authors take as confirmation of memory-bound generation.
7. **Quantisation accuracy (§5.4, Fig. 10).** Accuracy differences come mostly from architecture and size, not the quantisation scheme. Lower bit width means higher error. Models of **≤ 3B parameters were "unusable" at 3 bits** (hallucinating or repeating the prompt). No scheme was uniformly best; AWQ and GPTQ were slightly better for ≥ 7B models. Accuracy was evaluated on the pretrained, not chat, variants.
8. **Edge offloading (§5.5, Fig. 11, Table 6).** Jetson boards give higher and steadier throughput than phones ("Zephyr-7B (4-bit)" in the text, although Table 2 lists only Zephyr-3B: 3.3× prefill and 1.78× generation on average). Counter-intuitively, energy efficiency improves at **higher** power modes, attributed to memory clock scaling: the GPU stalls waiting for memory at low power. Orin AGX at 50 W, llama.cpp CPU: TinyLlama 0.0015 mWh/token, Llama2-7B 0.0204 mWh/token.
9. **Limitations (§6).** Chat models of 1–13B only (sub-billion models explicitly left out), device-level energy only, and quantisation as the only compression studied.

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **Independent mobile evidence for the prefill/decode split.** MELT confirms with real devices, power meters and profilers that generation is memory-bound and prefill is cheap per token ([[Prefill and Decode]]). GroundOne's workload is the cheap half only: one prefill and a readout at the markers, no generation. The thesis's own M4 ratio (decode ≈ 16× more expensive per token than prefill on the 0.5B, ≈ 14× on the 3B) is the laptop counterpart of MELT's Fig. 3 gap.
- **Energy per generated token vs zero generated tokens.** MELT's 0.58–0.76 J per generated token for a 3B 4-bit model on a phone gives a scale for what a generative judge pays per token. GroundOne 3B spends 33.1 J for a whole answer of K ≥ 14 sentences on the M4 (SoC power, net of idle). That equals the energy of roughly 44–57 generated tokens at MELT's phone rates, i.e. about 3–4 generated tokens per sentence ([[Energy of LLM Inference]], [[GroundOne Results]]). Measurement scopes differ (whole phone with screen at 25% brightness vs M4 SoC only; chat vs verification), so this is an order-of-magnitude illustration, not a comparison.
- **Power is not energy.** MELT's phones draw up to 13.8 W sustained during inference, while total energy is set by how long the decode loop runs. This mirrors the GroundOne finding that the System One draws more power (16.2 W) than the decoding judges (11–14 W) but uses less energy because it finishes sooner.
- **Quantisation choice.** The finding that ≤ 3B models degrade badly at 3 bits, and the 4-bit vs 3-bit speed result, support keeping GroundOne's small models at **8-bit** rather than pushing bit width down ([[Quantization]]). MELT did not test 8-bit, so this is indirect support.
- **Apple silicon data points.** The M2 Max at 46.8 tok/s (Llama-2 7B 4-bit) and the Metal advantage on iPhones are useful context for [[Apple Silicon and MLX]] and [[Local Inference Hardware]]. MELT did **not** benchmark MLX: it is mentioned only as desktop-only at the time (§7).
- **Not applicable.** No sub-billion models, no encoder or classifier workloads, no prefill-only use case, and no laptop measurements. The thermal and battery problems that MELT reports for continuous chat matter less for a verifier that runs one short prefill per answer. That is an argument, not a measurement.
