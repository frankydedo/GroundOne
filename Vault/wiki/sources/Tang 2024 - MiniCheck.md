# Tang 2024 - MiniCheck

**Raw file**: `raw/Tang 2024 - MiniCheck_ingested.pdf` (30 pages; read pp.1–10, 15–24)

MiniCheck shows that **small, specialized fact-checkers** trained on carefully built synthetic data can match GPT-4 at checking whether a sentence is grounded in a document, at about 400× lower cost. The paper also introduces the [[LLM-AggreFact]] benchmark. This is the closest prior work to GroundOne: a **sentence-level, binary supported/unsupported** checker, run per sentence, with no claim decomposition. Concept page: [[MiniCheck]].

## Overview
- **Task definition (§2)**:
  - A response is split into sentences c_i. Each sentence has grounding documents D_i.
  - A discriminator M(D, c) ∈ {0,1} outputs unsupported (0) or supported (1). A sentence counts as supported if *any* document supports it: max_j M(D_ij, c_i).
  - The NLI "contradiction" class is folded into unsupported, because contradictions are rare in the benchmark (footnote 2).
- **Synthetic data (§3, Table 1)**: GPT-4 builds hard examples where a claim must be checked fact by fact and across multiple sentences.
  - **C2D** (claim→doc): 7,076 examples from about 400 Wikipedia claims, 42% negative. It decomposes each claim into atomic facts, expands each fact into a sentence pair, generates a document, then removes one sentence to make an unsupported variant. Subclaims are paired by power-set.
  - **D2C** (doc→claim): 7,319 examples from about 300 Google News articles from Nov 2023 onward, 65% negative. It summarizes chunks, then augments by removing sentences and by pairing a claim with other chunks of the same document.
- **Models (§3.3, Table 3)**:
  - **MiniCheck-RBTA**: AlignScore's RoBERTa-L, 355M, trained on 14K synthetic examples.
  - **MiniCheck-DBTA**: DeBERTa-v3-L, 355M (as given in the paper's Table 3).
  - **MiniCheck-FT5**: Flan-T5-L, 770M.
  - DBTA and FT5 use 35K examples: 14K synthetic plus a **21K subset of ANLI** made of the examples that fooled the models during ANLI's collection. ANLI *neutral* and *contradiction* are mapped to **unsupported**. Training on more ANLI did not help.
- **Training (App. G)**: standard cross-entropy. Models are first trained on everything except D2C for 2 epochs (bs 4, grad-accum 2, lr 1e-5 for RoBERTa and 5e-5 for the others), then on D2C for 1 epoch (lr 1e-5). Ending on human-written D2C documents "adapts back" to realistic text.
- **Decision rule (§3.3)**: predict supported if the score is above t. **The default is t = 0.5, the midpoint of the score range**, with no per-dataset tuning.
- **Long documents (App. E.2)**: documents are split into chunks at sentence boundaries: about 400 tokens for RBTA and DBTA, **500 whitespace tokens for FT5**. The final score is the maximum over chunks.

## Key results
- **LLM-AggreFact test, no threshold tuning (Table 2, p.7), average BAcc**: MiniCheck-FT5 **74.7**, MiniCheck-RBTA 72.7, MiniCheck-DBTA 72.6, AlignScore 70.4, SummaC-ZS 67.9, QAFactEval 66.5. For the LLMs: GPT-4 75.3, Claude-3 Opus 74.1, Mistral-Large 73.4, GPT-3.5 69.6.
- **With per-dataset threshold tuning on dev (Table 7, App. B.1)**: MiniCheck-FT5 75.1 and AlignScore 72.2. MiniCheck-FT5 drops only 0.4 points without tuning, while SummaC-CV drops 9.2. The authors read this as robustness that comes from the synthetic data.
- **Cost (Tables 3–4)**: about **$0.24** to run MiniCheck-FT5 over the 13K test set, at $0.8 per A6000 GPU-hour. GPT-4 costs $107 for the same set.
- **Ablation (Table 6)**: training on the ANLI subset alone ("-BOTH") falls to 59.9 average BAcc without tuning, 14.8 points below the full model. Removing C2D costs 2.0 points and removing D2C costs 3.2.
- **Decomposition and decontextualization (Table 5, §7)**: splitting claims into atomic facts gives no consistent gain (GPT-4 +0.3, FT5 −1.4) and costs 2–4× more compute. GPT-4 decontextualization gives +0.0.
- **Synthetic label quality (App. A.3)**: 80% (C2D) and 78% (D2C) agreement with adjudicated human labels on 40+40 samples. Fleiss' κ is 0.51 and 0.70.

## Limitations (as stated by the authors, pp.9–10)
- There is no explanation and no error localization.
- The benchmark does not test reasoning over evidence spread across documents.
- The models are English-only.
- The synthetic data is generated by GPT-4, with the noise that brings.

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **The task definition is directly reusable**: per sentence, binary supported/unsupported, with neutral and contradiction both mapped to unsupported. That is exactly GroundOne's output space, read out as two answer letters.
- **Training recipe template**: about 35K examples (ANLI hard subset plus synthetic C2D/D2C) is enough for a 770M model. The C2D/D2C data is released per the abstract ("We release LLM-AggreFact, code for data synthesis, and models"). **Its licence is not stated in the paper.**
- **Baselines to beat**: MiniCheck-FT5 at 74.7 (paper) or 75.0 (current leaderboard) on [[LLM-AggreFact]]. GroundOne's Qwen2.5-0.5B is a smaller decoder model than Flan-T5-L (770M encoder-decoder), so matching it would be a strong result.
- **Evaluation protocol to copy**: no per-dataset threshold tuning (t = 0.5) as the headline number, with dev-tuned thresholds as a secondary analysis. See [[Groundedness Verification]].
- **Context limit**: MiniCheck itself chunks at 400–500 tokens and takes the maximum. GroundOne, with Qwen2.5's long context, may avoid chunking, but should report how it handles inputs longer than its training length.
- **How to run**: code at `github.com/Liyan06/MiniCheck` (footnote 1); checkpoints are linked from the leaderboard ([[Tang 2024 - LLM-AggreFact Leaderboard]]). Apple-Silicon support is not stated.
