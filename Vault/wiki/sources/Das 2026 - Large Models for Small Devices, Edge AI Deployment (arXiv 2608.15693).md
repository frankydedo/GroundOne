# Das 2026 – Large Models for Small Devices: Recent Advances and Empirical Analysis of Edge AI Deployment

**Raw file**: `raw/local_inference/Large Models for Small Devices, Edge AI Deployment (arXiv 2608.15693)_ingested.pdf`

## Overview
A 28-page **survey plus empirical study** whose thesis is that "what compresses well, however, need not deploy well" (abstract). The survey part reviews more than twenty works that report compression results **measured on real constrained hardware** (pruning, quantization, tensor decomposition, distillation; mostly vision models, §3). The empirical part deploys three ~1B LLM families (Qwen3.5 0.8B, Gemma 3 1B IT, TinyLlama 1.1B; Qwen2.5 1.5B Instruct as an auxiliary model) with structured pruning, GGUF quantization and LoRA recovery on an RTX 4070 laptop GPU, a Ryzen 9 7940HS CPU and a Raspberry Pi 4B (8 GB), all served with llama.cpp, on SQuAD, BoolQ and Natural Questions, plus six segmentation models (§4–5). The conclusion: "No single technique wins across tasks … The right technique depends on the task, the model, and the hardware."

## Key Insights
1. **Quantization arithmetic (§3.2).** "A 7B-parameter model needs 28 GB in FP32 but only 7 GB in INT8 and 3.5 GB in INT4", which is often what makes a model tractable on an edge device. Storage, memory footprint and bandwidth scale linearly with bit-width.
2. **Quantization results (§5.1.4).** Qwen3.5 0.8B at **Q5_K_M** (551 MB) reaches **93.85 SQuAD F1 / 92 EM** and 75% BoolQ; Q4_K_M (508 MB) lowers its quality mean from 59.68 to 54.92. For TinyLlama and Gemma, Q4_K_M gives the *best* quality mean: quality is "non-monotone in bit width", and quantization "changes generation behavior rather than merely degrading numerical fidelity".
3. **Hardware, not size, sets latency (§5.1.1).** At similar artifact sizes (508–769 MB) the mean latency is 0.13 s/sample on GPU, 0.61 on CPU and 8.91 on the Raspberry Pi (about 4.8× and 70× slower).
4. **Pruning can make deployment worse (§5.1.2, §6.1).** Removing as little as 1% of MLP channels costs substantial quality in all three families; LoRA recovers only part of it and unevenly across tasks (§5.1.3). Pruned widths break the 256-element k-quant super-block alignment, so the Q4_K_M files **grow** by 21% (Qwen3.5), 33% (Gemma) and 49% (TinyLlama); pruned models also become more verbose. Raspberry Pi latency rises up to 3.4×.
5. **Prefill/decode decomposition (§6.3, Eq. 13, Fig. 5).** $T_{e2e} = \mathrm{TTFT} + (N_{gen}-1)\,\mathrm{TPOT}$. "Prefill consumes the whole prompt in a single parallel pass and is compute-bound. Decode then emits one token at a time, and because every step must re-read the full weight set from memory, its cost is governed by memory bandwidth rather than arithmetic." Measured over 18 matched baseline-vs-pruned pairs (18,000 requests): per-token decode time rises in all 18 (by 0.5–30%), while prompt evaluation moves by at most +16%; the slowdown "accumulates in decode, exactly where edge inference is already most constrained".
6. **Prior collapse on binary tasks (§6.2).** A compressed model can remain fully parseable while collapsing onto one class. Qwen2.5 1.5B with LoRA after 1% pruning predicts "yes" on **97 of 100** BoolQ items, keeps **71% strict accuracy** but has **52.6% balanced accuracy**; no collapsed configuration exceeds 54.3% balanced accuracy, and strict accuracy can rank two collapsed models in the wrong order. Recommendation: report parse rate, prediction skew and balanced accuracy, not strict accuracy alone.
7. **Deployment recommendations (§7, Table 10).** For binary QA use cases ("yes/no command validation … local policy-compliance assistant, FAQ triage") check balanced accuracy and prior collapse. For chat, prefer quantization and output-length control over structural pruning (Q4_K_M when memory dominates, Q5_K_M when quality does). Model selection should be repeated after every deployment stage (§7.2).
8. **Motivation and open directions (§1, §8).** Local inference "offers low latency and privacy without connectivity" (§1). Future work: an optimisation framework that **profiles prefill and decode separately** to choose among KV-cache optimisation, speculative decoding, prompt caching, token pruning and early exit, especially where "memory bandwidth and decoding latency dominate" (§8).

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **Where GroundOne sits.** A small (0.5B/3B) model, quantized (8-bit), adapted with LoRA, deployed locally: the "compact LLM on edge hardware" setting of §4, but on an Apple M4 laptop (unified memory, MLX) rather than llama.cpp on a GPU, CPU or Raspberry Pi. GroundOne uses no pruning, which this paper argues is the riskiest lever for LLMs.
- **Strongest citation for the prefill/decode argument.** §6.3 states plainly that prefill is a one-off compute-bound pass and decode is a per-token, memory-bandwidth-bound loop, and measures that edge slowdowns accumulate in decode. A model that **only prefills** (GroundOne design (b): one forward pass, zero generated tokens) avoids the phase the authors identify as the edge bottleneck, and also avoids the verbosity and format-drift effects they measured (§5.1.2) ([[Prefill and Decode]]). Caveat: their measurements are on llama.cpp generative QA, not on a classifier readout; the thesis must not present them as measuring GroundOne's regime.
- **Supports reporting balanced metrics.** §6.2 is an independent, recent example of the failure mode that motivates GroundOne's balanced decision and per-class reporting: a three-way verifier that collapses onto "supported" could look accurate on skewed test sets. Their recommendation (parse rate, prediction skew, balanced accuracy) maps onto GroundOne's readout, which has no parse failures by construction (restricted letter logits) ([[Letter-Token Readout]], [[Constrained Decoding]]). Note: Qwen2.5 appears here only as a pruned 1.5B auxiliary model, not the GroundOne configuration.
- **Quantization nuance.** The non-monotone effect of bit width (§5.1.4) supports the thesis's choice to evaluate the *deployed* 8-bit model rather than assume equivalence with full precision ([[Quantization]]).
- **Verification/hallucination.** Not discussed. Binary QA is listed as an edge use case (Table 10), the closest the paper comes to decision-style outputs; all models still generate their answers as text.
- **Quality caveats.** Preprint; the survey part is thin (about twenty works, mostly CNNs). Evaluations use small samples (100 items per task in the reported BoolQ/SQuAD comparisons), and the authors themselves flag uncontrolled prompt templates across Raspberry Pi campaigns (§5.1.1–5.1.2). Its strengths are its own measurements, open code, and candour about confounds.
