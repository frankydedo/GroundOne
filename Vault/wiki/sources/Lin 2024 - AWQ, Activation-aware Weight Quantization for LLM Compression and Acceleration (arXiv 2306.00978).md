# Lin 2024 – AWQ: Activation-aware Weight Quantization for On-Device LLM Compression and Acceleration

**Raw file**: `raw/local_inference/Lin 2024 - AWQ, Activation-aware Weight Quantization for LLM Compression and Acceleration (arXiv 2306.00978)_ingested.pdf`

## Overview
A **low-bit, weight-only** (W4A16 / W3A16) post-training quantisation method aimed explicitly at **on-device** LLMs, motivated by lower cloud cost, offline use and privacy (abstract, §1). The observation: only **0.1–1 % of weights are salient**, and they are found by looking at **activation** magnitudes, not weight magnitudes. Instead of keeping them in fp16 (mixed precision, hardware-unfriendly), AWQ **scales the salient input channels up** before quantisation and folds the inverse scale into the previous operator. No backpropagation or reconstruction is used, so it needs little calibration data and overfits less than GPTQ. The companion runtime **TinyChat** turns the 4× smaller weights into **3.2–3.3×** average measured speedups over Hugging Face fp16 on desktop, laptop and mobile GPUs (§1, §6). AWQ is widely integrated (Transformers, TensorRT-LLM, vLLM, and others; §1).

## Key Insights
1. **Salient weights by activation (§3.1, Table 1; INT3, group 128, WikiText perplexity).** OPT-6.7B: fp16 10.86, RTN 23.54. Keeping 1 % of channels in fp16 **chosen by activation magnitude** gives 11.39; chosen by weight norm 22.37; random 24.23. OPT-1.3B: RTN 119.00 → 16.91 (1 %, by activation) vs 98.55 (by weight).
2. **Scaling instead of mixed precision (§3.2, Eq. 1–5, Table 2).** Multiplying a weight by s > 1 and dividing its input by s leaves the rounding error roughly unchanged but shrinks the **relative** error of that weight, as long as the group maximum (and therefore the scale Δ) does not change. On OPT-6.7B, scaling the 1 % salient channels by s = 2 improves perplexity from 23.54 to 11.92; larger s (4) hurts again (12.36) because it inflates the error of the other channels. AWQ searches s = s_X^α per input channel with a 20-point grid over α ∈ [0, 1], plus weight clipping (§3.2, §5.1).
3. **Accuracy vs RTN and GPTQ (Table 3–5, 8).** INT3-g128 OPT: AWQ 16.32 / 13.58 / 11.39 / 10.56 / 9.77 for 1.3B–30B vs RTN 119.47 / 298.00 / 23.54 / 46.04 / 18.80. Llama-2-7B INT4-g128: fp16 5.47, RTN 5.73, GPTQ 5.69, GPTQ-R 5.63, **AWQ 5.60**; INT3-g128: 6.66 / 6.43 / 6.42 / **6.24**. Llama-2-70B INT4: 3.32 → 3.41. Mistral-7B: 4.14 → 4.30 (INT4) / 4.83 (INT3); Mixtral-8x7B: 5.94 → 6.05 / 6.52. Generation tasks at INT4-g128: GSM8K Llama-2-7B 13.87 (fp16) vs 13.57 (AWQ), 11.07 (RTN), 12.13 (GPTQ); MBPP pass@1 CodeLlama-7B 38.53 vs 40.64.
4. **Less calibration data, less overfitting (§5.3, Fig. 8; OPT-6.7B, INT3-g128).** AWQ reaches better perplexity with 16 calibration sequences than GPTQ with 192. Calibrating on PubMed and evaluating on Enron (or vice versa) costs AWQ +0.5–0.6 perplexity, GPTQ +2.3–4.9.
5. **Why weight-only helps on device: roofline (§4.1, Fig. 3; Llama-2-7B fp16, RTX 4090, batch 1).** Prefilling a **200-token prompt takes 10 ms**, generating **20 tokens takes 310 ms**. The 4090 offers 165 TFLOPS and 1 TB/s, so anything below 165 FLOPs/byte is memory-bound; fp16 decode has arithmetic intensity ≈ **1**, prefill ≥ 165. Weight traffic dominates activation traffic by orders of magnitude, so 4-bit weights raise decode intensity to ≈ 4 FLOPs/byte and its ceiling about 4×.
6. **TinyChat (§4.2, §5.4, Fig. 9–10, Table 10).** On-the-fly dequantisation fused into the matmul kernel (MM and MV), SIMD-aware 4-bit packing (up to 1.2× on ARM NEON), kernel fusion of LayerNorm/QKV/positional embedding and in-kernel KV cache updates. Results: 2.7–3.9× over HF fp16 on the 4090 (Llama-2-7B: 52 → 62 tok/s from fp16 fusion alone, then a further 3.1× from 4-bit kernels); Llama-2-13B at **33 tok/s on an 8 GB RTX 4070 laptop GPU**, where fp16 cannot even fit 7B; up to 1.7× over llama.cpp on Jetson Orin; Llama-2-70B on a 64 GB Orin; 7B at 0.7 tok/s on a Raspberry Pi 4B. VILA-7B/13B accelerated by up to 3.1× / 2.9×.
7. **Benchmark protocol (§5.4).** Batch size 1, a **4-token prompt**, 200 generated tokens, median latency. The speedups are therefore **decode** speedups; prefill is essentially absent from the measurement.

## Relevance to GroundOne
- **The cost model, measured by someone else.** Fig. 3 is the cleanest external statement of the thesis's [[Prefill and Decode]] argument: on a 4090, one decode step (310/20 ≈ 15.5 ms) costs as much as prefilling about 310 prompt tokens (10 ms / 200 tokens = 0.05 ms per token). The thesis's own MLX measurement on the M4 gives a much smaller ratio, about **17–21 prompt tokens per decode step**. Arithmetic on these two numbers shows the M4 is far less compute-rich relative to its 120 GB/s bandwidth than a 4090, so on the M4 a long prefill is *relatively* expensive. That is why GroundOne's advantage from skipping decode is real but bounded (≈2× over the terse judge at K ≥ 14; [[GroundOne Results]]).
- **AWQ's speedup does not apply to a System One workload.** The roofline argument says weight-only quantisation raises the ceiling of **memory-bound** decode; prefill is already compute-bound (intensity ≥ 165) and stays so, and TinyChat's speedups are measured with a 4-token prompt. GroundOne's design (b) is one prefill and no decode, so 4-bit AWQ would cut memory (≈0.5 → ≈0.25 GB of weights for 0.5B, ≈3.3 → ≈1.7 GB for 3B, scaling the figures in [[Quantization]] and [[LoRA]]) but would not be expected to speed it up much. Dequantisation inside the matmul adds work on the compute-bound path. Not measured in the thesis.
- **Where AWQ would matter: the baselines and the generator.** Generative judges and the RAG generator itself decode, so they gain from 4-bit weights. A fair comparison keeps the same 8-bit format for GroundOne and the judges, as the thesis does.
- **Activation awareness and the logit readout.** AWQ's finding that the channels with large *activations* carry the important weights echoes [[Dettmers 2022 - LLM.int8(), 8-bit Matrix Multiplication for Transformers at Scale (arXiv 2208.07339)|LLM.int8()]]'s outlier analysis. MLX's 8-bit affine format (group size 64, from the base models' `config.json`) is plain round-to-nearest per group, without activation-aware scaling; at 8 bits RTN with small groups is usually considered safe, but AWQ's own tables are at 3–4 bits and do not prove that. The defensible claim for GroundOne remains an empirical 8-bit vs bf16 check on the A/B/C probabilities ([[Letter-Token Readout]], [[Calibration]]).
- **Calibration-set robustness as a design argument.** AWQ's robustness to the calibration distribution (Fig. 8) is relevant if GroundOne ever moves to 4 bits: GroundOne's inputs (retrieved documents plus answers with " Verdict:" markers) differ from generic pre-training text, and a reconstruction-based method like GPTQ would be more exposed to that shift.
- **On-device framing.** AWQ motivates on-device LLMs with cost, offline operation and privacy, the same pillars as the thesis ([[On-Device Inference]], [[Privacy and Data Sovereignty]]). A MacBook (Apple M1) appears among TinyChat's target platforms in Fig. 1, but the paper reports no Apple-silicon numbers in the text.
